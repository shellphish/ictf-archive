Preparation:

We first identified a set of high-level tasks for both attack and defense. On the attack side, there was network scanning, launching exploits and searching for the flag once a host is compromised. For defense, we had to work on defeating the scans from the other team, hiding the flags and detecting the attacks from the other team using network-based and host-based methods.

The next step was dividing the work among the team-members. It was decided that each team will work on some elements of both attack and defense, since those who are working on a particular attack are the also the best people to defend that attack.

Following the above methodology, the team was broken down into the following four sub-groups:

Group A: Exploits and Network-based Intrusion Detection
Group A's task was to prepare various exploits to break into the Alpha team hosts. The group was also responsible for preparing network-based intrusion detection for the exploits, mainly in the form of Snort signatures.
Group A report

Group B: Network Scanning
Group B's task was scanning the Alpha team's network and hosts, and reporting the information to Group A. On the defense side, this group was responsible for defeating the scans of the Alpha team i.e. making it hard for them to locate our defended hosts.
Group B report

Group C: Flag Hiding and Searching
Group C's task was to find effective ways to hide the flags on the defended hosts. They were also responsible for finding the flags of the enemy team once their machines were compromised.
Group C report

Group D: Host-based Intrusion Detection
This was the only group without any attack responsibilities. The group's job was to used host-based intrusion detection techniques to detect the Alpha team's attacks.
Group D report

Each sub-group report provides details about the group's preparation, execution and evaluation.

At the team level, multiple meetings were organized for the entire team as well as for just the group leaders. The aim of these meetings was to brainstorm on various issues, help the whole team understand what each group was doing, and to enable co-ordination between groups.

A private testbed was created at the residence of William Robertson. The purpose of this testbed was to test the Omega team's attack and defense software in a safe environment (i.e. where the enemy team has no opportunity to snoop).

On the evening before the exercise, the Omega team conducted a rehearsal of the entire exercise. An Instructional Computing laboratory was reserved for this purpose, and the rehearsal was executed on the private testbed mentioned above.
 

Lessons Learned:

The Omega team did not perform as well as it could have in this exercise. There are many lessons learned from this. Each sub-group has described their learnings in detail within their reports. At the team level, we learned one very important thing: communication and co-ordination are key elements of the success of any group effort. Although the sub-groups each prepared themselves thoroughly, the lack of sufficient co-ordination between them created a lot of confusion on the final day. As team leader, I learned how hard it is to manage the members of such a large team and get them to "listen" to one another.
