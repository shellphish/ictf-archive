CS 290I Network Security and Intrusion Detection - Red Team / Blue Team Exercise

The class is divided into two teams. The Alpha team is composed of the students with last names from A to N. The Omega team is compose of students with last name from O to Z.

When the exercise starts, the two teams will be assigned a set of hosts. The task of each team is to protect their hosts and attack the hosts of theother team.

At the beginning of the exercise, the teams have two hours of "truce" to prepare their hosts for the exercise (installation of attack/defense software, hiding of the flags, etc). The actual attck will be carried out in the following two hours.

The goal of the each team is to capture the flags, that is, to obtain files named secret.txt stored on each of the hosts managed by the opposing team. There could be multiple copies of the file. The only real flag is the file whose contents start with the keyword SECRET, other info is included in the file to discriminate among the different flags.

At the same time, each team has to protect the flags stored on their own hosts as much as possible but, more important, the task of each team is to detect the attacks performed by the other team. If an attack is detected than the team gets points.

Rules

There are several rules thaht must be followed prior and during the exercise. These rules are subject to change during the next couple of weeks to adjust to the requests and the requirements posed by the two teams.

  • The teams cannot use a priori knowledge about the testbed network.
  • The teams cannot disrupt services, bring down hosts, and delete files.
  • The teams cannot filter/block network traffic.
  • The teams cannot patch vulnerable software.
  • Each team decides, for each host, where to store the secret file. The only requirements are that the file must be on a mounted filesystem, the name is secret.txt, and only one copy of the file starts with the keyword SECRET. Other copies can be used as decoy but they cannot contain the secret string at the beginning of the file.
  • Only the hosts that will be given to you at the beginning of the exreicse can be attacked by the teams. Attacking other host will penalize SEVERELY a team.

Evaluation

Participation in the Blue Team/Red Team exercise is 20% of the final grade. Note that being able to successfully detect or carry out an attack is not the only important thing. Other aspects are evaluated:

  • Ability to plan in advance both attack and defense
  • Ability to deploy protection/detection systems and to prepare automated attack scripts
  • Ability to maintain a log of the activities (both attacks and detections). These logs will be gathered by the Team leaders and submitted in a final report.

Exercise Final Report

Format Overview

The Team Report for the Red-Blue Exercise MUST contain the following sections in the given order. The report MUST not exceed 6 pages in length.

  1. Team/Subgroup
  2. Mission
  3. Preparation
  4. Execution
  5. Evaluation
  6. Miscellaneous
  7. References

The report must conform to the structure described in the example report available here

Content

The following descriptions explain what MUST, MAY, or SHOULD be included in each section.

The Subgroup section MUST include the following things: the group name, a one line description of what the subgroup was supposed to do, the name of the group members, the email addresses of the group members, and the handles of the group members (on the test bed network). The group leader should be clearly marked.

The Mission section MUST include the following things: a paragraph giving a more detailed description of what the subgroup was supposed to accomplish, a concise list of responsibilities that each subgroup member had in achieving the subgroup's mission.

The Preparation section MUST include a description of the activities that were planned. In addition, The Preparation section MUST include a list of tools, software, methods, procedures, etc., that were used or independently produced by the subgroup to prepare for the exercise. Each contribution should be described briefly (1-2 sentences) and credit the specific author(s) if developed independently.

The Execution section MUST include a (course-grained) log of each member's activities,

The Evaluation section MUST include: an evaluation of how well the subgroup accomplished its mission, an evaluation of how well the subgroup executed the plan of attack, a description of what could have been done better, an evaluation of how well the subgroup interacted with any adjacent subgroups, an evaluation of the tools that were available for performing the subgroup's mission, and an evaluation of the exercise itself (eg, suggestions for improving the Red-Blue exercise).

The Miscellaneous section MAY contain any other information that the subgroup feels is pertinent.

The References section SHOULD contain a list of sources (eg, URLs, articles, books) that were most helpful in preparing for the Red-Blue exercise.
