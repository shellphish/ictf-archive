Group A was tasked with remotely exploiting Alpha team's defended hosts as well as writing network IDS rules to detect any incursions by Alpha into Omega's defended hosts.

Mission

Group A was reponsible for compiling a list of all known vulnerabilities against the expected target hosts, retrieving and modifying working exploits to circumvent naive network intrusion detection system rules, and provide a method for the flag search team to launch their search scripts on the target hosts.

In addition to penetrating Alpha's defended hosts, Group A was responsible for detecting attempted incursions against Omega's defended hosts, tailoring Snort to detect legitimate attacks while ignoring any spurious traffic launched by Alpha.

Preparation

Exercise Preparation

To prepare for the exercise, a private testbed for Omega consisting of one RH 7.0 and two RH 6.2 hosts was created in order to provide a private testing area where Alpha members could not snoop upon Omega's development activities.

Execution Plan

Once the IPs and open ports of Alpha's hosts were determined by the responsible group, Group A planned to execute its prepared obfuscated exploits through a desyncronizer designed to slip them past Alpha's NIDS undetected. Once a machine had been compromised, the search program, which had been adapted to run inside the exploited process without exec()ing, was to have been launched. Had it failed, it was to have created a backdoor. Should this entire process fail, backup exploits were to be executed and the search program manually downloaded to the target host.

General guidelines for the attack included maintaining emissions discipline during the exercise in order to decrease the chances of detection, and some proposed tactics included launching attacks only from UML virtual hosts or the third attacking host provided in order to decrease the visibility of the defended hosts.

To detect Alpha's exploit attempts, Snort was to be deployed on the defended hosts, utilizing a ruleset tailored to the environment and the range of attacks expected to be attempted by Alpha.

Evaluation

Group A did not perform as well as it potentially could have. Based on informal discussions with several Alpha team members, the tools developed by the group for the exercise would have allowed Group A to penetrate Alpha's RH 6.2 machine virtually undetected had several factors not prevented the group from satisfactorily setting up their attack tools. Regardless, the group was able to compromise Alpha's RH 6.2 host with standard, easily detectable exploits. The failure of Group A to penetrate Alpha's RH 7.0 host, however, was ultimately due to having come to the conclusion that it would not be breakable, without considering strongly enough the possibility that its services might be downgraded. The vulnerabilities were well researched, but the tools were not readily available when it became obvious that they were needed.

In hindsight, much could have been improved in both the preparation and execution stages. Too much time was spent on developing and not enough on the actual attack procedure, which prevented the group from performing as efficiently as it could have otherwise.

Another area ripe for vast improvements was that of group communication and interaction. Omega acted during preparation for the exercise as four separate cells, each acting independently despite the efforts of the team leader to foster communication between the groups. Had the groups cooperated earlier during the development process, much time would not have been wasted revising tools to integrate well with the rest of the team.

The only substantial criticism of the exercise itself that one could make is that there was not enough time to prepare. Perhaps this should have been recognized and the KISS principle applied a bit more strongly. Otherwise, the exercise was extremely fun to prepare for and itself nothing if not a learning experience to remember.
