Description and rules

In this exercise, the goal of each team is to obtain the maximum amount of information about a number of hosts. No attacks that will compromise the hosts can be performed. The goal of this exercise is to focus on scanning and scanning detection.

Some of the hosts to be scanned will be placed in a subnetwork under the instructor's control. Other hosts will be placed in parts of the testbed that are accessible to one team or the other.

Each team has the goal of scanning both the instructor hosts and the hosts of the other team. In addition, each team has to detect the attacks against the hosts under their control.

At the beginning of the exercise, the teams will have two hours of "truce" to prepare their hosts for the exercise (installation of attack/defense software, clock synchronization, etc). The actual attacks will be carried out in the following two hours.

At the end of the exercise each team has to submit the following information:

  1. For each group a short report on the exercise
  2. A single time-ordered truth file containing IDMEF alerts describing the attacks that have been performed.
  3. A single time-ordered detection file containing IDMEF alerts that represent the attacks that have been detected. For both the truth file and the detection file the AdditionalData tag in the alerts has to contain a tcpdump filter that can be used to extract the packets corresponding to the attack from a dump collected on the network.

Rules

There are several rules that must be followed prior and during the exercise. These rules are subject to change during the next couple of weeks to adjust to the requests and the requirements posed by the two teams.

  • The teams cannot use a priori knowledge about the testbed network.
  • The teams cannot disrupt services, bring down hosts, and delete files.
  • The teams cannot filter/block network traffic.
  • The teams cannot patch vulnerable software.
  • The team that uses the largest amount of traffic will be penalized.
  • Only the hosts that will be given to you at the beginning of the exercise can be attacked by the teams. Attacking other host will penalize SEVERELY a team.

Evaluation

Participation in the Blue Team/Red Team exercise is 20% of the final grade. Note that being able to successfully detect or carry out an attack is not the only important thing. Other aspects are evaluated:

  • Ability to plan in advance both attack and defense
  • Ability to deploy protection/detection systems and to prepare automated attack scripts
  • Ability to maintain a log of the activities (both attacks and detections). These logs will be gathered by the Team leaders and submitted in a final report.
  • Ability to detect attacks reliably.

Exercise Final Report

Format Overview

The Team Report for the Red-Blue Exercise MUST contain the following sections in the given order. The report MUST not exceed 6 pages in length.

  1. Team/Subgroup
  2. Mission
  3. Preparation
  4. Execution
  5. Evaluation
  6. Miscellaneous
  7. References

The report must conform to the structure described in the example report available here. Please use HTML. MS Word documents (or equivalent garbage) will be discarded.

Content

The following descriptions explain what MUST, MAY, or SHOULD be included in each section.

The Subgroup section MUST include the following things: the group name, a one line description of what the subgroup was supposed to do, the name of the group members, the email addresses of the group members, and the handles of the group members (on the test bed network). The subgroup leader should be clearly marked.

The Mission section MUST include the following things: a paragraph giving a more detailed description of what the subgroup was supposed to accomplish, a concise list of responsibilities that each subgroup member had in achieving the subgroup's mission.

The Preparation section MUST include a description of the activities that were planned. In addition, The Preparation section MUST include a list of tools, software, methods, procedures, etc., that were used or independently produced by the subgroup to prepare for the exercise. Each contribution should be described briefly (1-2 sentences) and credit the specific author(s) if developed independently.

The Execution section MUST include a (course-grained) log of each member's activities,

The Evaluation section MUST include: an evaluation of how well the subgroup accomplished its mission, an evaluation of how well the subgroup executed the plan of attack, a description of what could have been done better, an evaluation of how well the subgroup interacted with any adjacent subgroups, an evaluation of the tools that were available for performing the subgroup's mission, and SHOULD contain an evaluation of the exercise itself (e.g., suggestions for improving the Red-Blue exercise).

The Miscellaneous section MAY contain any other information that the subgroup feels is pertinent.

The References section SHOULD contain a list of sources (eg, URLs, articles, books) that were most helpful in preparing for the Red-Blue exercise.
