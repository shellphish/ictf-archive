    In this exercise, the Alpha and Omega team will compete in a scavenge hunt. The scavenge hunt goal is to break into a (simulated, yet realistic) payroll system and perform a money transfer transaction.

    Each team will have to perform a number of tasks (e.g., scan a network or break into a host). Each task must be completed in a limited amount of time (e.g., 30 minutes). The first team that achieves the task gets 5 points. If the other team completes the task within the specified time, it gets 3 points. If the time elapses and the team is not able to complete the task, then a cheat-sheet will be provided so that the task can be completed, but no points will be assigned.

    A task is disclosed only after the previous one has been completed by both teams. The teams will have to attack separate hosts/networks and therefore there is no gain in creating obstacles to each other.

    Bonus points may be assigned for particularly clever exploits/solutions to the tasks.

    No detection task will be required. Intrusion detection evasion may grant additional bonus points, though. At the end of the exercise, each team must provide a truth file in IDMEF form. Each attack that is carried out during the scavenge hunt should have a description in terms of IDMEF alerts.

    You will have a supervisor (one of the TAs) that will follow your action and may give you advice/suggestions about the correctness and fairness of your approaches.

    The team should organize into subgroups to explore and be proficient in the following tasks:

    • Network scanning techniques, in particular stealth ones
    • Attacks against MySQL servers, both locally and remotely. Also ability to change data in a MySQL database
    • NIS-based and NFS-based attacks
    • Buffer overflows (local, remote) and privilege escalation techniques in general
    • Password cracking techniques
    • Attacks against Apache web servers, CGI applications
    • Vulnerability scanning

    Creating obstacles to the other team is considered cheating. Spying on the other team is considered cheating. Generating huge amounts of traffic will be penalized.

    Rationale

    It is important that the rationale for this exercise be made clear.

    The ultimate goal is to perform a multi-step attack that is as realistic as possible. This means that using any domain knowledge or trying to bypass tasks will impact a team's score.

    The setting in which you will have to operate is mostly unknown but will contain standard services, sometimes secured, sometimes unpatched. You may be required to analyze small snippets of code to find vulnerabilities on the fly.

    Tcpdump data, BSM data, Windows event logs, and Snare events will be collected during the scavenge hunt. Combined with your truth file this will be an invaluable resource for other researchers in the field of intrusion detection and attack correlation. I'm not overestimating the impact of this exercise: if everything goes well, this will be the first realistic dataset of this kind.

    Nothing like this has been tried before. I encourage your constructive critiques.
