WHAT: The UCSB International Capture The Flag (iCTF) Hacking Contest (ictf.cs.ucsb.edu)

WHEN: March 22nd, 2013 from 8am PDT to 5pm PDT

WHO: Teams from educational institutions around the world 

HOW: Subscribe at http://www.ictf2013.info

WHY: Because you need to prove you're the best!

THE THEME, AND HOW IT ALL WORKS:

Welcome to the National Cyberdefense Corps! You, along with other
qualified applicants, have just been hired to secure the critical
infrastructure of your country.

With the globally recognized threat of Sicilian hackers on the
offensive, the need to defend your country's critical services from
catastrophic compromise is more urgent then ever.

To facilitate this defense, you will be give privileged access to a
server that runs a number of critical infrastructure services. Like
all software, these services might have a number of undiscovered and
undisclosed vulnerabilities.

These services run your nation's backbone, and therefore are used by
citizens of your country on a regular basis. As part of the normal
operation of a service, a piece of secret, service-specific
information, called a "flag", is stored and utilized by each service.

Intelligence has deduced that, amidst this normal, benign traffic,
there are attacks launched by those sneaky Sicilian hackers! These
attacks exploit vulnerabilities in the services in order to retrieve
flags that, under normal circumstances, would not be accessible to the
attacker.

Your first duty is mitigation. Using connection netflow information,
provided by Intelligence, you must identify any connections that
contain an attack. Each connection will be identified by a unique ID,
the source IP and port, the destination IP and port, and a
timestamp. A successful identification of a malicious connection will
allow Intelligence to mitigate the damage that the attack might cause,
and recover points for your country. Missing an attack, or incorrectly
identifying intended usage of a service as an attack, will penalize
your country, reducing points.

In order for the mitigation to be successful, the attack must be
identified quickly. After a few hours, the detection is worthless.

Your next goal is vulnerability management. As the only line of
cyberdefense, you must identify vulnerabilities in your country's
services and, if possible, patch their services so that they are not
vulnerable. Note, however, that if patching makes the service
non-functional for the users (that is, it does not work as expected
when used in a benign way), the service will be labeled as
"non-functional". If the service is not responding at all, the service
will be labeled as "down". The amount of services that are fully
functional directly affect the points that are assigned for correct
detections. More precisely, the historical values of availability
creates a multiplier that is applied to the overall score.

Of course, little know to your commander-in-chief, the hat that you
wear isn't entirely white. It's more like an off-white or grey. A
hacker's gotta eat (and eat in style).

The way you earn your extra money (points) is to develop exploits and
sell them to the Zebra Dwarf Institution, a sort of vulnerability
clearinghouse.

It is well known that the ZDI may use and run your exploits against
other nations. The ZDI requires that the exploit be written according
to a well-defined Python-based framework (soon to be published in
underground forums).

ZDI gives money for submitting a valid exploit, and they give
residuals for each successful flag captured from a service.

Note that, to prevent tarnishing your country's reputation, you can't
launch attacks against other nations. Instead, ZDI (the organizers'
infrastructure) will attack each team separately, using both known
exploits, and the exploits submitted by the various teams.

If the exploit written by nation X is successfully run against a
service of nation Y, points are given to nation X. If the attack is
detected by nation Y, of course, points are also assigned to nation
Y. Therefore, teams should devise stealthy attacks, and, at the same
time, effective patching and attack detection.

A scoreboard shows, in real-time, the status of the services of each
team, and the amount of points accumulated. Note that the points can
decrease, because of false positives.

ZDI is very picky about the code they run. After all they are the ones
risking to get blown out of a building by a drone!  Therefore, ZDI
assumes that the code produced by you will be "well-behaved" (no DOS,
no attacks against the execution/scoring infrastructure). If such
behavior is detected in the attack code, ZDI will retaliate against
the offending nation to disastrous results.

The people of your country place their trust in you. Don't let them down!
