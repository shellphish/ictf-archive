Now, some notes about scoring. Read carefully.

The score for a team is a composition of four elements:

* The Service Level Agreement (SLA), which takes into account which
  services are up and/or uncompromised during the competition.

* The Timeliness score, which provides a bonus to teams that exploit a
  service earlier in the game.

* The Diversity score, which provides a bonus to teams that exploit
  larger numbers of services.

* The Leetness score, which provides points based on the number of
  teams that are exploiting a specific service.

More details about these components follow.

* Service Level Agreement

During each tick in the game (say, 2 minutes), the scorebot determines
the status of the services for each team.

A service can be down (that is, a TCP connection cannot be
established) or non-functional (that is, something prevents the
service from being used as intended. For example, a flag cannot be set
or retrieved). In both cases, the team who owns the service gets 0
points for that service (but might be informed of the type of failure,
to help diagnosing problems).

A service can be functional (which implies it is up). In this case, if
the service has been compromised the team who owns the service gets 1
SLA point for that service. If the service was not compromised the
team gets 2 SLA points. The overall SLA is the average of the SLA
values across the whole competition.

For example, suppose that there are three services (S1, S2, S3) and
three ticks (there will be a lot more, of course). These are the value
for a specific team.

Tick 1: S1 is down = 0, S2 and S3 are up and uncompromised = 2 + 2 =
4. Total is 4

Tick 2: S1 is up but non-functional = 0, S2 is up and compromised = 1,
and S3 is up and uncompromised = 2. Total is 3

Tick 3: S1 is up and uncompromised = 2, S2 and S3 are down = 0. Total
is 2

At the end of the competition, the average SLA is (4 + 3 + 2) / 3 = 3.

At the end of the competition, the overall SLA is determined relative
to the other teams. That is, the team who has the highest SLA (say 6)
gets a score of 1. Everybody else gets a score that is proportional
to the highest value. For example, the team in the example above has
an SLA of 3 and therefore would get a score of 3/6 = 0.5.

This team’s final SLA is 0.5, which means that the overall score of
 this team (computed as a composition of timeliness, diversity, and
 leetness) will be cut in half.

Needless to say, it is very important that the team works hard to have
their services up and functional (and possibly uncompromised).

* Timeliness    

The timeliness points are given to teams who submit early a working
exploit to a service.

The first team gets full points (e.g., 10), the second team gets less
points (e.g., 5), and so on. After a few iterations, no one else
receives timeliness points for that service.

This point system gives a bonus to the fastest team to submit a
working exploit (and penalizes those who copy it after having observed
it on the wire).

At the end of the competition, the overall timeliness score is
determined relative to the other teams. That is, the team who has the
highest timeliness points (say 50) gets a score of 100. Everybody else
gets scores proportional to the highest value (for example, a team
that has 10 timeliness points would get a score of 10/50*100 = 20).

The timeliness score accounts for 20% of the overall score.

* Diversity

The diversity points are given to teams in proportions to the number
of different services they successfully exploit. If a team exploits a
service, it will get 10 points. When the team submits a second
service, the team will get 20 points (for a total of 30 points), for
the third one, the team will get 30 points (for a total of 60 points).

At the end of the competition, the overall diversity score is
determined relative to the other teams. That is, the team who has the
highest diversity points (say 400) gets a score of 100.  Everybody
else gets scores proportional to the highest value.

The diversity score accounts for 40% of the overall score.

* Leetness

Leetness points are given to the teams that are able to exploit the
most difficult services.

At each tick there is a number of points (say 100) that are in
play. These points are divided equally among the services that are
being exploited in that tick. For example, if there are 5 services
being actively exploited, each service will get 20 points. These 20
points are shared equally among the teams that are actively exploiting
the service. So if a service is exploited by 2 teams, they will get 10
points each. If a service is exploited by 5 teams, they will each get
4 points each.

At the end of the competition, the overall leetness score is
determined relative to the other teams. That is, the team who has the
highest leetness points (say 2344) gets a score of 100.
Everybody else gets scores proportional to this value. 

The leetness score accounts for 40% of the overall score.

* Summary

In summary, suppose that a team has an SLA of 0.5.

Suppose that the maximum amount of point for timeliness is 700 (that
is, these are the timeliness points of the most timely team) and a team got
30 timeliness point. Their normalized timeliness score is then
30/700*100 = 4.28.

Suppose that the maximum amount of point for diversity scored by a
team is 210 (that is, these are the diversity points of the most
diverse team) and that the team exploited 5 services (150 diversity
points). Their normalized diversity score is 150/210*100 = 71.4.

Finally, suppose that the team has gained 150 leetness points, and the
best performing team gained 310 leetness points. Therefore their normalized
leetness score would be 150/310*100 = 48.38

The overall score of the team would be (4.28 * 0.2) + (71.4 * 0.4) +
(48.38 * 0.4) = 48.78. These is because of the relative weights of the
scores.

This has to be me multiplies by the SLA. The team has an average SLA
of 5.4, while the team with the best SLA has an average SLA of
7. Therefore the actual SLA score would be 5.4/7 = 0.77, and the final
point would be: 48.78 * 0.77 = 37.63.
 
The team who has the highest overall final score wins.  Note that
there might a few parameter that we might change in order to balance
the game, but this should be a pretty realistic description of our
scoring procedure.
